Brief Introduction

Satellite photogrammetry offers low-cost and broad-coverage Digital Surface Models (DSMs), but building boundaries often suffer from edge-fattening caused by dense matching smoothness constraints. Fixed-buffer DSM post-refinement can miss local fattening widths or include nearby ground objects, while height-similarity refinement may create abrupt and unnatural transitions.

SRAD addresses these issues with two connected designs. Height Potential Contour Tracing (HPCT) adaptively delineates fattening-edge buffers from building footprints and DSM heights. Gradient Consistent Refinement (GCR) then optimizes heights within these buffers using plane labels and gradient consistency, preserving smooth topographic transitions while correcting building edges.

Problem
Fattening edges blur building boundaries and contaminate nearby ground in satellite DSM products.
Delineation
HPCT traces adaptive interior and exterior buffers using a height potential field, attraction force, and elastic force.
Refinement
GCR replaces height similarity with gradient consistency to support smooth, continuous height transitions.

Framework of SRAD

1. Input priors
Initial satellite DSM, DOM/image guidance, and building footprints define where edge correction should be considered.
2. HPCT
Interior and exterior contours deform on the height potential surface until they delineate adaptive buffers.
3. GCR
Plane labels are optimized inside the adaptive buffers and merged back into the original DSM.

The method separates where to refine from how to refine: HPCT estimates adaptive buffers and GCR updates only the selected edge regions.

Takeaways

SRAD is designed for DSM post-refinement rather than dense matching regeneration. Its advantage comes from explicitly aligning refinement regions with building footprints and DSM height transitions, then refining only the adaptive buffers with gradient-consistent height labels.

The experiments verify the contribution of adaptive buffer delineation, elastic contour stabilization, and second-order refinement under different footprint and refinement settings.
